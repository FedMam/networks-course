Athens, the capital of Greece, is one of the world's oldest cities, with a history spanning over 3,400 years. It is often regarded as the cradle of Western civilization and the birthplace of democracy. The city is named after Athena, the goddess of wisdom and warfare, who was believed to have offered the olive tree to the Athenians as a gift.

Historically, Athens rose to prominence in the 5th century BCE during the Golden Age, particularly under the leadership of statesman Pericles. This period saw the construction of iconic structures such as the Parthenon on the Acropolis, which symbolizes the city's cultural and political achievements. Athens was also a center for philosophy, arts, and sciences, home to great thinkers like Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle.

After the decline of classical Athens, the city experienced various occupations, including Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman rule. In the 19th century, Athens became the capital of the newly independent Greek state. Today, it is a vibrant metropolis that blends ancient history with modern life, attracting millions of visitors each year to its archaeological sites, museums, and lively neighborhoods.
