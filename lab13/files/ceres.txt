Ceres: The Largest Asteroid & a Dwarf Planet

Located in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, Ceres holds a unique distinction. Discovered in 1801, it was initially classified as a planet, then the largest asteroid. In 2006, it was officially recognized as a dwarf planet – the only one within the inner solar system.

Ceres is a rocky and icy world, large enough for gravity to pull it into a nearly spherical shape (about 590 miles/940 km in diameter). NASA's Dawn spacecraft orbited Ceres from 2015 to 2018, revealing a complex world with a heavily cratered surface, mysterious bright spots (identified as salt deposits), and evidence of a significant amount of water ice, possibly even a subsurface brine layer. This suggests Ceres could be a relic ocean world, offering clues to the solar system's history and the potential for water-rich environments beyond Earth.
